**6. VaR / ES of the $1.30 risk unit.**

The stop caps the loss at $1.30 plus fees and the slippage beyond the stop. With continuous 24/7 5m data a resting stop never opens through its level at 5m resolution (gap share 0.000 in all 5 assets, 2,600+ hits). So **the tail of the risk unit is the stop slippage (WS10 / WS17), not the return distribution.**

## Dossier 1: the variable and why we model it

**The target is the conditional variance of the next bar's log return**, at three horizons: 1h, 4h and 1d. Bars are built on their own grid: hourly bars, 4h bars starting at 00/04/../20 UTC, and UTC days. Every forecast is made at the bar's open, using only bars that have already closed.

Each horizon feeds a decision:

| use | where in the code | horizon | what goes wrong if the spec is poor |
|---|---|---|---|
| stop distance (0.5-4 sigma cap, stops.MAX_STOP_SIGMA, H-STOP1 prior) | execution/gate.py, research/stops.py (sigma x close) | the strategy's bar: 1h, 4h | a sigma that is too low in loud hours puts stops inside the noise; one that is too high in quiet hours wastes the R budget |
| EV gate (min_net_expected_r, costs in R) | execution/gate.py (sigma_risk per bar) | 1h, 4h | costs are converted to R with sigma; a biased sigma biases every net-R number |
| sizing of the $1.30 risk unit (risk.yaml live_small.risk_to_stop_usd) | at-risk, gate | stop distance in sigma | the realised loss per trade is $1.30 x (fill distance / stop distance) |
| live network base scale s2 = sigma_risk_next^2 | live/nn.py, ml/hourly.py (y = r / sqrt(s2)) | 1h | the network learns corrections to s2; a better s2 leaves less for it to fix |
| central / first-passage estimates (sigma_rb_next) | tools.vol, market_state | 1h..1d | TP / stop-hit probabilities |

**The proxy is realised variance** (RV): the sum of squared 5m log returns over the bar (12, 48 or 288 returns). It is an unbiased, noisy proxy of quadratic variation. QLIKE ranks forecasts consistently under an unbiased noisy proxy (Patton 2011); MSE does too, but it weights the loud bars much more heavily.

The return that the stop actually sees is the close-to-close log return r of the bar. The distribution dossier (WS03 core) standardises r by each forecast: z = r / sqrt(F).

What this says about the specification:

- **Stationarity.** For crypto, log RV rejects a unit root (ADF) at every horizon, and KPSS also rejects stationarity at p = 0.01. Both rejecting is the classic long-memory signature: the level is persistent (fractionally integrated) but mean-reverting. It is the case for HAR (a long-memory approximation), FIGARCH and the rough-vol kernel, and against a short-memory GARCH(1,1).
- **Metals.** The short metals history passes KPSS (p 0.10). Their ACF is dominated by the weekly cycle (lag 168h 0.70, lag 7d 0.80), because weekends are almost silent. Any calendar-blind model mis-forecasts every weekend there.
- **Level dependence.** Returns have little linear dependence (Q(20) 21-140 on 43k bars) and very strong dependence in squares (Q 5k-36k). Vol clustering is the dominant structure.
- **Tails.** Hourly excess kurtosis is 14-44 (unconditional). How much is left after standardising by each forecast is the WS03 question below.
- **Jumps.** 11-15% of quadratic variation is jump variation (1 - BV/RV) at 1h-4h, and about 9% at 1d.
- **Parameter stability** (clock log-HAR re-fit per calendar year, HAC SEs; properties.json):
  - BTC 1h: the sum of the slopes (persistence) runs 0.99 (2022), 0.96, 0.92, 0.95, 0.96 (2026). The 1h lag runs 0.26-0.34 (SE 0.015), the 24h lag 0.25-0.36, the 168h lag 0.09-0.18; the 720h lag is not significant.
  - ETH 1h: persistence 0.90-0.99, the same shape.
  - BTC 1d: persistence 0.73-0.87, and the 30-day lag is never significant.
  - The coefficients drift by 2-4 SEs from year to year, but the shape is stable. Monthly expanding refits capped at 3 years are a reasonable compromise.
  - The clock range (quietest / loudest cell) is stable at about 0.4-0.5x / 2.3-2.5x by hour of week.

## Dossier 3: candidate specifications and their assumptions

| spec | information set | dynamics | distribution | stationarity / linearity | why plausible here | what it implies if true |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| rbc (live sigma_risk) | one squared return per bar, winsorised at 4 sigma, bias-corrected | EWMA lambda 0.94 (IGARCH, no mean reversion) | none (point) | random-walk variance, calendar-blind | robust, cheap, validated vs plain EWMA (H-V1b) | vol is a martingale and every hour is alike |
| rbc_how | rbc + hour-of-week shape | EWMA x periodic factor | none | multiplicative seasonality | 0.5x-2x hourly shape (H-GEO1, H-NS4) | vol = level x clock |
| ewma | squared returns | EWMA 0.94 | none | IGARCH | RiskMetrics default | as rbc, without robustness |
| GARCH(1,1) N / t / skew-t | squared returns | mean-reverting, short memory (geometric decay) | Normal / Student-t / Hansen skew-t innovations | covariance-stationary if alpha + beta < 1; symmetric | the textbook default (vol.garch_t in ARCHITECTURE 3.1) | shocks decay geometrically; one return per bar carries all the information |
| GJR-t, EGARCH-t, APARCH-t | squared returns | + leverage (sign asymmetry); EGARCH in logs; APARCH power delta | t | as GARCH | equity-style leverage; crypto asymmetry is weak and unstable | down moves raise vol more than up moves |
| FIGARCH-t | squared returns | hyperbolic (long-memory) decay, d in (0, 1) | t | long memory | the KPSS + ADF pattern above | shocks persist for weeks |
| gjr_tc | clock-deflated squared returns | GJR on r / sqrt(f), x f | t | periodic GARCH (Andersen-Bollerslev 1997) | intraday GARCH without deseasonalising is mis-specified | GARCH is fine once the clock is removed |
| HAR / HARQ / HAR-CJ (levels) | 5m RV (BV, RQ) at 3-5 lags | linear cascade (Corsi); HARQ shrinks the short lag when RQ is high (measurement error); HAR-CJ separates jumps | none (OLS) | linear in levels; heteroskedastic errors | realised measures carry more information than one squared return | vol is a cascade of heterogeneous horizons |
| log-HAR | log RV | linear in logs, Duan smearing back-transform | lognormal-ish errors | log-linear, much less heteroskedastic | log RV is near-Gaussian, and the repo's validated vol.har (H-VCL1, H-VM5) | multiplicative shocks |
| loghar_c | log of clock-deflated RV | log-HAR on RV / f, x f | as log-HAR | periodic multiplicative | combines both facts above | the clock is a separate, stable multiplicative layer |
| loghar_cr | + release dummy | + a shift in bars with a scheduled CPI / NFP / FOMC | as log-HAR | as log-HAR | release minutes carry ~15x variance (H-GEO3) | macro releases are known jumps in the level |
| loghar_cny | NY-local clock (DST-aware) | as loghar_c | as log-HAR | as log-HAR | the US cash open follows NY time (H-GEO6) | US hours drive the clock |

**Not run** (lean mode after the 23:40 UTC compaction): MS-GARCH, Realized GARCH, component GARCH, SV, RFSV, a realised kernel from flow_3s.
- Prior evidence: RFSV ties log-HAR on QLIKE at 1h/4h/24h (H-RFSV2 inconclusive). SV on log RV ties log-HAR at 1d (vol tournament, sv_rv in the in-sample MCS). MS-GARCH is WS07's (regime) remit.
- These are named as open below, not as tested.

## Caveats

- **The holdout was used before.** The clean holdout (2026-07-01 ->) had already been used for related vol comparisons:
  - daily HAR family through 2026-11-10 (H-VM1..5);
  - robust_bc x hour-of-week through 11-10 (H-NS4);
  - RFSV through 11-15 (H-RFSV2);
  - the XGB next-hour model (H-NN8).

  So for rbc, rbc_how and log-HAR this is a re-measurement, not a confirmation. The new comparisons are: the clock log-HAR at 1h/4h, the GARCH family on intraday bars (raw and clock-deflated), release dummies, the NY clock, and the innovation-distribution and 4-sigma tail tests. The "fresh" slice (origins >= 2026-11-16, about 10 days) is descriptive only.
- **RV from 5m returns is a noisy proxy**, especially at 1h (12 returns). QLIKE rankings are robust to unbiased proxy noise; loss levels are not. No microstructure correction was needed at 5m for these liquid perps. A realised kernel from flow_3s was not computed.
- **Metals are short.** XAU/XAG history starts 2026-03-09, and evaluation starts in May (1h/4h) or June (1d). The in-sample segment is 1-2 months, so metals verdicts are holdout-dominated, low-power and descriptive. GARCH at 1d is not estimable there (fewer than 300 daily bars).
- **Survivorship.** The five crypto perps are today's large survivors.
- **The sample is a run of regimes, not iid.** The in-sample period is 2023-01 to 2026-06: a bull run with two drawdowns. The holdout is about 5 months.
- **No transaction costs.** This is a forecast-loss tournament; nothing is traded. The decision link is the size of the sigma used by stops, the EV gate and the network.
- **GARCH estimation choices.**
  - Zero mean, returns x 100, arch 8.0 defaults.
  - Warm starts from the previous month; a failed fit reuses the previous month's parameters (counts in `fails_<sym>_<grid>.json`).
  - No sanity filter was applied to GARCH paths. The registered design had none, so the EGARCH / APARCH level drift seen in the BTC 4h smoke test is left in.
- **Mincer-Zarnowitz in logs.** The Wald test of (a, b) = (0, 1) rejects for every spec. E[log RV] = log F + a negative Jensen term, so the intercept is not zero even for a perfect forecast. Only the slopes b (and R2) are informative. This is a design flaw; it was noticed after the results and left as run.
- **The stop-gap measure is degenerate.** With continuous 24/7 5m bars, the first 5m bar to touch a resting stop always opens above it, so the realised R is 1.0 by construction (gap share 0.000). Losses beyond the stop are slippage (WS10 / WS17), not modelled here.
- **The H-WS04-5 VaR rule.** Its Kupiec condition is two-sided, so the in-sample over-coverage (0.82%) counts as a failure. The holdout under-coverage (1.44%) fails on its own.
- **EGARCH-t at 1d.** The variance path collapses on shock days (ETH 2023-12-06: F / RV = 2e-6; QLIKE 82 in-sample). This is the known degenerate optimum; it was not filtered and is reported as is.
- **Disclosure.** A one-symbol pipeline smoke test (BTC 4h, segments pooled) printed mean losses before the full run. Nothing in the design was changed after it.

## Method

- **Code.** `src/ai_trader/research/vol_spec.py`, with tests in `tests/test_vol_spec.py` (7). Stages: `download | build | forecast | evaluate | dist | passage | properties | extras | tables | verdicts | record | report`.
- **Runtime.** Forecasts took 3 governor jobs of 13-26 min each, single-threaded (peak 297 MB), after queue waits of 2-22 min. Evaluate took 13 s, dist 64 s, passage 10 s, extras 111 s, properties 61 s.
- **Storage.** data/research/ws04 holds 52 MB; the 3.5 MB raw download was deleted after the build.
- **Data.** Bybit linear 5m klines (public REST):
  - BOS cache 2022-01..2026-11-15;
  - intraday_geometry extension to 2026-11-25 22:00;
  - our extension to 2026-11-26 23:40 (data/raw/WS04, deleted after processing);
  - XAU/XAG from 2026-03-09.
- **Grids.**
  - Hourly RV = sum of squared 5m log returns, rescaled when 10-11 of the 12 returns exist; null below 10.
  - BV = (pi/2) sum |r_j||r_{j-1}|; RQ = (M/3) sum r^4.
  - 4h and 1d bars sum the hours, and need >= 90% of them.
  - Returns are close-to-close per bar.
- **Forecasts.**
  - Bar i is forecast at its open from bars < i.
  - Refits on the first of each month: expanding window capped at 3 years; training pairs whose target closed before the refit.
  - Evaluation origins from 2023-01-01 (crypto), 2026-05-01 (metals 1h/4h) and 2026-06-01 (metals 1d).
- **Clock.** For each refit, the cell mean of min(RV / slow, 50) on training bars, shrunk with kappa 20 toward the grand mean, circularly smoothed at 1h, normalised to mean 1.
  - The slow baseline is the longest HAR component known at the bar's open (720 h / 180 4h-bars / 30 days).
  - rbc_how uses RV / rbc at the 1h level, summed over the bar's hours at 4h / 1d.
- **Release dummy.** A CPI, NFP or FOMC scheduled time inside the bar (data/research/news/events/events.parquet), counted only if the schedule was known at the bar's open.
- **Losses and tests.**
  - QLIKE = RV/F - log(RV/F) - 1, and MSE (levels).
  - DM: Newey-West HAC (Bartlett, automatic lag) with the Harvey-Leybourne-Newbold correction; p from t(n-1).
  - MCS: Hansen-Lunde-Nason Tmax, 90%, circular block bootstrap (blocks of 48 h / 12 4h-bars / 10 days), B = 1000. It runs on the common sample of all specs: per asset, and for pooled crypto = the cross-asset mean loss per origin over common origins.
  - Mincer-Zarnowitz: log RV on log F, HAC Wald of (0, 1).
- **Selection rule (frozen).** The simplest spec in the pooled-crypto in-sample 90% MCS that is also in the holdout MCS. Simplicity order: rbc < rbc_how < rbc1h_flat < ewma < har < loghar < loghar_c < loghar_cr < loghar_cny < harq < harj < GARCH family. Fallback: the in-sample winner.
- **Distribution (WS03 core).**
  - z = r / sqrt(F). Normal, unit-variance Student-t and Hansen skew-t (arch distributions; location 0, scale fitted) fitted by ML on llm_in_sample z.
  - Holdout per-observation log score, with DM-HLN on the differences.
  - Kupiec POF, Christoffersen independence / CC, PIT KS, Berkowitz LR.
  - GPD: POT beyond the 5% quantile of each tail (scipy genpareto, location 0).
  - 4-sigma tail: one-bar P(z < -4) and P(z > 4), with a binomial interval around the model probability.
- **First passage.**
  - Origins at 00/04/../20 UTC (00 UTC for 24 h). Touch of -k sigma_1h (long) or +k sigma_1h (short) by 5m lows / highs within H hours.
  - Model: an H-step t(nu_1h) random walk with the horizon spec's variance and the Broadie-Glasserman-Kou continuity correction; the Normal reflection formula is reported for comparison.
  - Resting long stop: fill at the stop, or at the 5m open if that bar opens through it. R loss = fill distance / stop distance.
- **Risk unit.** Loss in $ = 1.30 x R loss (fees excluded). For an unprotected bar, the loss is 1.30 x (-z) / k for a stop k sigma away.
