# H-X1: close-confirmed vs resting structural stops (2026-11-07)

**Hypothesis (pre-registered):** exiting only on a 1h close beyond a structural stop (plus a resting
catastrophe stop at 2x the distance) gives higher net mean R than a resting stop hit by any intrabar touch.
**Success:** paired B − A > 0 with t > 2 in-sample; same sign in holdout; worst R under B ≥ −2.5.

**Verdicts recorded:** `llm_in_sample` = **contradicts**; `clean_holdout` = **inconclusive**.

## Method (src/ai_trader/research/stops.py)
- Signals: `donchian_breakout` and `ema_stack` from the registry with default parameters, computed on the full BTCUSDT 1h history. A trade opens at the 1h close where the signal's sign changes to nonzero, and the strategy exits at the first later close where the sign differs.
- Stop: most recent pivot low (long) or high (short) that is confirmed causally (k = 3, so usable from bar i+3) and sits on the protective side of the entry. Add a buffer of 0.3·σ·close, where σ = `sigma_next` at entry (a one-bar 1h log-return forecast, ≈ fractional move). Skip the trade if the distance is outside [0.5, 4]·σ·close. There is no target.
- A: resting stop on 5m bars. The fill is the stop, or the 5m open if the bar gapped through it.
- B: exit at a 1h close beyond the stop. A resting catastrophe stop sits at 2x the distance, using the same 5m fill rules.
- Costs: 7.5 bps per side (taker 5.5 + slip 2). R = pnl / (|entry − stop| + entry·15 bps), the same as at-risk sizing.
- Segments are split by entry time at the knowledge cutoff 2026-07-01. The holdout ends at the end of 5m coverage (2026-09-27). Trades still open at that date are dropped.
- Per strategy, in-sample pass requires: diff > 0, min(simple t, NW t) > 2, and worst R_B ≥ −2.5. Holdout pass (n ≥ 8) requires: diff > 0 and worst R_B ≥ −2.5. A segment verdict is **supports** only if both strategies pass and **contradicts** if neither passes.
- In-sample numbers were computed and recorded before the holdout was read.

Supplementary result, ETHUSDT with 1h bars only (coarse: a resting stop counts as touched by the 1h high/low, with a worst-case fill). Not recorded:
- In-sample: ema_stack n=232, B−A −0.001 (t −0.02); donchian n=10, B−A −0.150 (t −1.85).
- Holdout: ema_stack n=18, B−A +1.58 (t 0.91), driven by one large trend.

Caveats:
- The [0.5, 4]σ band removes 95% of donchian entries: breakouts sit a median 6.3σ from the last pivot. It also removes 65% of ema_stack entries (median 4.8σ). The donchian sample is therefore tiny.
- About 52–64% of resting-stop exits closed back inside the stop within the same hour. That matches the motivation for the hypothesis. However, B's losers are larger: about 13% of B trades hit the −2R catastrophe stop, and close-confirmed exits fill beyond the stop. This cancels the saved whipsaws.
- Both policies have negative mean R on these unfiltered entries, so this is a test of the stop policy, not of the strategies' edge.

## Conclusion
Waiting for a 1h close does not beat a resting stop on BTC in-sample. The difference is slightly negative (−0.05R per trade) and far from significant for both strategies. The holdout (10 trades) points the other way but is too small to count. The registered success criterion fails.

**Implication for at-exec:** keep the current resting SL, the Bybit position stop with a MarkPrice trigger. Do not switch to close-confirmed stops with a catastrophe stop. Close-confirmed exits roughly double the worst-case loss (about −1.9R against −1.0R) and gain nothing on average, and they need the strategy process to be alive at every hour close. Using MarkPrice as the trigger already filters last-price wicks, which is a cheaper version of what this hypothesis was aiming for. Revisit only if a larger, cleaner holdout shows B − A > 0.
