## Verdicts in plain words

1. **Use a pegged limit, not a taker order, for $50-200 entries on every liquid perp. It saves about 2.3-2.5 bp per order.** (H-WS10-2 **supports**, both segments.)
   - Setup: a $100 PostOnly limit at the touch, re-pegged each second, with a 60 s timeout and then a taker fallback, simulated on the real ms tape with a pessimistic queue.
   - All-in cost against the decision-time mid: **3.1 bp (BTC, ETH), 3.5 (SOL, XRP), 3.8 (DOGE)**.
   - The same order as a taker costs **5.5 / 5.5 / 6.1 / 5.9 / 6.2 bp**.
   - Fill within 60 s: 93-98%. The median wait is a few seconds.
   - Taker minus pegged, day-cluster 95% CIs:

     | symbol | gain (bp) | 95% CI |
     |---|---|---|
     | BTC | +2.34 | [+2.23, +2.43] |
     | ETH | +2.32 | [+2.27, +2.37] |
     | SOL | +2.53 | [+2.46, +2.58] |
     | XRP | +2.45 | [+2.39, +2.50] |
     | DOGE | +2.46 | [+2.38, +2.53] |

   - The gain is positive in every symbol-hour.
   - The pre-cutoff days give the same gain (+2.34 to +2.57 bp).
   - Exception, when the price runs away: in the 10-13% of decisions where the mid moves at least 1 sigma in our direction within 60 s, the peg costs 4.4-7.4 bp. That beats the taker on BTC, ETH and XRP but loses on DOGE (-0.9 bp) and is level on SOL.

2. **The harness's flat slippage numbers are conservative for liquid perps.** (H-WS10-3 **supports**.)
   - Taker slippage beyond the touch-mid at $100 is 0.01 bp (BTC), 0.03 (ETH), 0.62 (SOL), 0.47 (XRP) and 0.77 (DOGE). The harness charges 2.0 bp.
   - Stop-market slippage vs the stop level (300 ms latency, all three stop distances pooled) averages 0.8-1.6 bp. The p99 is 10-18 bp. The harness charges 10 bp on the liquid tier.
   - The one real stop (BTC, 2026-11-20) slipped 3.0 bp. That is the model's 94th percentile: plausible, on the unlucky side.

3. **Own price impact is negligible on the majors but not on mid-cap alts, if the square-root fit is taken at face value.** (H-WS10-1 **contradicts** by the letter.)
   - At $200, predicted impact plus book walk is below 10% of the all-in taker cost in only 48% of the 552 symbol-hours.
   - On the five majors it is under 8% (BTC 0.03 bp, DOGE 0.5 bp).
   - On mid alts it is 10-30% (1-3 bp on ETC, FIL, ATOM, DOT, XLM, LTC).
   - This number extrapolates an *aggregate-flow* square-root curve to a single tiny order (Q / 60 s volume ~ 1e-4). Because the curve is concave it is steep near zero, so this is an upper bound.
   - The book walk itself is zero at $200 everywhere (the order is inside L1).

4. **Funding is the cost the harness leaves out, and it matters for zoo_ensemble.** (H-WS10-4 **contradicts**; H-WS10-5 **contradicts**.)
   - Mean funding paid per closed trade:
     - zoo_ensemble: 20.5 bp = **0.037 R** (long-biased trends held a median 6.7 days in mostly positive funding);
     - trend_4h: 8.5 bp = 0.011 R;
     - hrb2: -2.4 bp = -0.004 R (its shorts receive funding).
   - Re-priced on **closed trades only**, with a stationary day bootstrap whose block = max(5, p90 holding days) (RT-STRESS-1/2, orchestrator instruction).

5. **Impact specifications: which to trust.**
   - **Aggregate impact is close to a square root in signed flow** (H-WS10-8 **supports**). The concave spec `dm / sigma = kappa * sign(x) |x/V|^beta` beats the linear scaled one out of sample (pooled DM t = 12.3 at 60 s; every symbol t >= 4.2). beta is 0.42-0.52 at 60 s (day-bootstrap CI 0.40-0.56; it contains 0.5 for BTC, ETH and XRP). beta rises with the horizon: 0.26-0.46 at 10 s, 0.54-0.68 at 300 s.
   - A constant dollar Kyle lambda explains 9-29% of 60 s mid changes out of sample (H-WS10-6 **supports**). But it is unstable: the Chow F across days is 35-120, and DOGE's lambda does not transfer to the pre-cutoff price level (R2 -0.78).
   - Scaling a *linear* lambda by trailing sigma / volume does not help (H-WS10-7 **inconclusive**, pooled t -0.4). It helps SOL and hurts BTC.
   - **Book order-flow imbalance (Cont-Kukanov-Stoikov OFI) beats trade flow** at 10 s (pooled t 46, R2 0.64-0.75) and at 60 s (t 20). At 300 s there is no difference (H-WS10-9 **supports**). OFI plus flow is best at every horizon.
   - **Impact is transient** (H-WS10-11 **supports**):
     - order signs have long memory, with gamma 0.24-0.48 (CI < 1 everywhere);
     - a free 200-lag propagator beats permanent-only sign impact out of sample (pooled DM t 9.8, every symbol t >= 2.4);
     - the kernel peaks 2-10 orders after a trade and decays to 40-65% of the peak by 200 orders (SOL, XRP, DOGE) or 70% (BTC).
     - The parametric power-law kernel fit is degenerate (it sits on the grid boundary), so the efficiency relation beta = (1 - gamma) / 2 is not identified.

6. **Spread decomposition.**
   - Makers at the touch lose before fees: the 60 s realized spread is -0.69 to -0.75 bp (notional-weighted), with the CI below 0 on all five perps. The maker-side markout at 60 s is -1.0 to -1.6 bp per pegged fill.
   - The Huang-Stoll three-way model is mis-specified on these 1-tick books: alpha is outside [0, 1] on about 100% of symbol-days. So H-WS10-14 is **inconclusive**: the realized-spread leg holds, the model leg fails.
   - The Roll estimator is invalid (H-WS10-15 **supports**). It is undefined on 18-81% of symbol-days, and is 2.5-4.9x the effective spread on BTC and ETH.

**What to use.**
- For costs, use `cost_inputs.parquet` (below).
- For short-horizon impact, use OFI + flow (K5), or the square-root aggregate spec when only trades are available.
- Use a propagator kernel, not permanent impact, when chaining orders.
- Use the measured realized spread for maker adverse selection.

**What to stop using.**
- A constant dollar Kyle lambda across days or price levels.
- The Huang-Stoll and Roll decompositions on Bybit perps.
- The harness's omission of funding for multi-day trend holds.

**Suggestions for the rules owner (research/rules.yaml was not edited):**
- R-EXE-1 holds at $50-200 and extends to XRP and DOGE.
- Add funding to every multi-day net-R number.
- On DOGE / SOL runaway entries, a taker fallback before 60 s would be cheaper. That is a hypothesis to test, not a rule.

## The cost table (`data/research/ws10/cost_inputs.parquet`)

Columns:
- `mean, p50, p90, p95, p99, n`;
- components `fee_bp, hs_bp, walk_bp, drift_sd_bp, impact_bp`;
- pegged: `fill_60s, fill_5s, t_fill_med_s, mk5_bp, mk60_bp, fallback_share, runaway_mean_bp, runaway_share, prorata_mean_bp, taker_paired_bp`;
- stops: `slip_mean_bp, slip1s_mean_bp`;
- `source`: `ms_tape` (5 PART perps), `flow3s_proxy` / `flow3s_structural` (the other 18 stress perps).

**Validation.**
- The FIT-day distributions cover the TEST days well. Coverage of the FIT 90% quantile on TEST is 0.88-0.90 for the pegged cost and 0.83-0.88 for stop slippage; coverage of the 99% quantile is 0.97-0.99.
- The level is stable: pegged mean FIT vs TEST 3.13 / 3.17 bp on BTC, 3.43 / 3.59 on SOL.
- Real fills (data/journal/live, deduplicated; no balances read or written):
  - two BTC maker entries slipped 1.32 and 0.57 bp vs the mid and filled in 17.7 s and 9.2 s. That is the model's 75th / 64th percentile for slippage and 83rd / 69th for time to fill;
  - one ZEC maker entry slipped 1.93 bp (not in the tape sample);
  - one real BTC stop slipped 3.0 bp, the model's 94th percentile.
  - The other "exits" are manual closes (CreateByClosing). Their slip vs the stop is not a stop slippage.
- The paper book has only 2 maker entries on PART days, both at +1.31 bp vs the mid. That matches the harness's 1.3 bp pegged slip and our 1.0-1.8 bp pegged chase.

**How the alt rows were made (the parts that are proxies).**
- Pegged: the 3 s flow_3s proxy is level-calibrated on the five perps (raw proxy minus its mean bias; leave-one-symbol-out mean absolute error 0.25-0.36 bp per symbol-hour). The OLS slope (0.43, r 0.53) is attenuated by proxy noise, so it was not used to extrapolate.
- Taker: half-spread from the 3 s time-weighted spread, a linear-book walk from the 25 bp depth, and a drift tail from 3 s vol × 1.29 (the 1 s / 3 s ratio measured on the five).
- Stops: **the registered 3 s stop proxy failed calibration** (r 0.29 vs the ms tape; the 3 s close is too coarse). It was replaced by a labelled structural model: alt half-spread + the five perps' slippage in excess of their half-spread, scaled by relative 3 s vol (≥ 1).

## Dossiers (condensed)

**D1. Taker and pegged implementation shortfall (bp).**
- Decision: entry order type, the cost term of every net R.
- Properties:
  - Taker cost on 1-tick books is degenerate: fee + half a tick (0.007 bp BTC) + 1 s drift. The drift is symmetric with sd 0.9-1.3 bp and fat tails (p99 of total about 8-10 bp).
  - Pegged cost is bimodal: fills at about 1-2 bp of chase plus 2 bp fee, and 2-8% fallbacks at taker cost after a runaway. That makes it right-skewed (p99 10-17 bp).
- Specs:
  - empirical simulation on the ms tape, with pessimistic vs pro-rata queues (pro-rata is lower by only 0.04-0.07 bp, so the queue model is not decisive);
  - the 3 s proxy for alts.
- Out-of-sample: FIT -> TEST quantile coverage as above.
- The decision (peg > taker) does not change across queue models, segments or hours.

**D2. Stop slippage.**
- Decision: stop-loss R, gap risk.
- Measured: first print at or through levels set at 0.5 / 1 / 2 trailing-1h sigma from each hour's open; fill = the exit-side touch 300 ms later (and 1 s later).
- Mean 0.8-1.6 bp, fat right tail (p99 10-18 bp). It does not grow with the stop distance (k 0.5 / 1 / 2: 0.65 / 0.84 / 0.97 bp on BTC).
- The 1 s latency variant is similar.
- Sample: about 1,800 stops per symbol on TEST days.

**D3. Aggregate impact (60 s mid change on signed flow).**
- Decision: impact term for larger orders; which state variable to watch.
- Properties: 60 s mid changes are fat-tailed and heteroskedastic. Flow / volume is heavy-tailed.
- Specs:
  - K0 zero;
  - K1 constant lambda;
  - K2 sigma/V-scaled linear;
  - K3 sigma-scaled power;
  - K4 OFI / L1 depth;
  - K5 OFI + scaled flow.
- All fit on FIT (pre-cutoff + 10-06..10-29), scored on TEST (10-30..11-24) with MSE and pinball. DM uses Newey-West lag 10 with the HLN correction, pooled per timestamp across symbols.
- Ranking at 10 s and 60 s: K5 > K4 > K3 > K2 ≈ K1 > K0.
- Stability: day-level lambda CV 0.35-0.52.
- Trust K3 / K5. The pre-cutoff check repeats the concavity (K3 > K2) on every symbol. OFI (K4) beats K2 there except on ETH and DOGE at 60 s and 300 s.

**D4. Event-time impact (propagator).**
- Sign autocorrelation C(1) = 0.30-0.57, C(100) = 0.01-0.04, power-law decay gamma 0.24-0.48.
- Free 200-lag kernel by Toeplitz solve vs permanent-only, exponential and power-law kernels. Free wins out of sample.
- R2 for the next-order mid change is small (BTC 0.085, DOGE 0.32): most of the variance is not trade-driven.

**D5. Spread components.**
- Effective half-spread vs the pre-trade mid, notional-weighted: 0.45 (BTC) to 1.70 bp (DOGE).
- Realized spread at 5 s and 60 s is negative everywhere. Price impact at 60 s exceeds the effective spread.
- The Huang-Stoll model's constant-spread / linear assumptions fail on 1-tick books (alpha < 0).

**Power.**
- With about 74,000 pegged decisions per symbol on TEST (26 days), the day-cluster CI half-width of the pegged-vs-taker gain is about 0.05-0.1 bp. Effects of 0.2 bp are detectable.
- The re-pricing is not power-limited on costs: the 0.037 R funding shift is exact arithmetic. It is power-limited on the strategy edge itself: the zoo_ensemble CI width is about 0.7 R (RT-STRESS-2).

## Caveats and what went wrong

- **Virtual orders.** The pegged and stop simulations use the 1 s book state (up to 1 s stale) and the ms taker tape. Our orders never change the book, and queue position comes from the 1 s L1 size.
- **Drift is unconditional.** The taker 1 s drift is unconditional, with mean about 0. A momentum signal's entries would see a positive conditional drift: R-EXE-2 measured 1.1-1.6 bp of chase.
- **Impact extrapolation.** H-WS10-1's impact at $200 extrapolates an aggregate fit. The propagator's per-order theta (0.05-0.43 bp per average order) is a second, also upper-ish, estimate.
- **Alt rows are proxies** calibrated on five symbols over 13 overlap days. The 3 s stop proxy failed, and a structural replacement was used (labelled deviation, not registered).
- **H-WS10-5 was tested on closed trades with matched blocks** (orchestrator / WS01 RT-STRESS instruction), not on all trades with 5-day blocks as in the registered stress design. This is a labelled modification. G1 (cliff) and G3 (crisis) are carried over from the stress run, and the lat5 and fee gates use the model costs.
- **TEST days overlap PART's HELD days.** PART used them for other targets (returns, vol, maker adverse selection), not for impact or costs.
- **Operational problems:**
  - the first passes hit governor memory caps (2-worker pool, the flow_3s scan) and were rerun with one worker;
  - the first `prop` / `spread` outputs were debug runs on partial days and were rerun on all 310 symbol-days before recording;
  - the session was paused 00:40-07:12 UTC by the API limit.
- **Not done (orchestrator compaction):** VPIN / PIN and the queue-reactive model.

## Method and data

- **PART days:** 62 UTC days × 5 Bybit USDT perps (BTC, ETH, SOL, XRP, DOGE); 81.7 M taker orders; 1 s ob200 book states. FIT = 12 pre-cutoff days + 2026-10-06..10-29; TEST = 2026-10-30..11-24.

- **Other inputs:** the saved stress trades (data/research/stress/trades), lake funding settlements, data/journal/live fills, the paper book fills.

- **Runtime:** day pass 289 s (310 symbol-days, 1 worker), flow pass about 6 min, fits 3-5 min each. Governor queue waits up to 15 min while other agents held the heavy slots.
